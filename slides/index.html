Section 01

What are Agentic CLIs?

This section builds the foundational mental model. The key shift: you stop writing every instruction and start declaring outcomes.

Generative AI vs. Agentic AI

Generative AI

  • You ask → it answers
  • Produces content: text, code, images
  • Stateless — forgets between sessions
  • No ability to act on your environment
  • Example: ChatGPT web chat, Gemini.google.com

Agentic AI

  • You declare a goal → it figures out the steps
  • Plans, executes, observes, corrects
  • Uses tools: files, shell, web, APIs
  • Recovers from errors autonomously
  • Example: Gemini CLI, Claude Code, Aider
The key distinction: generative AI produces content; agentic AI uses that content to take actions. You are now a director giving outcomes, not a typist giving instructions.

How an Agent Actually Works

Under the hood, every agentic CLI follows the same basic loop:

Think
"What do I need to do?"
→
Do
Use a tool
→
Check
"Did that work?"
→
Repeat
Until done
This is why it can fix its own mistakes — it sees the error, thinks about what went wrong, and tries a different approach. No human needed in the loop.

Example: "Set up a Node project with tests" → creates files → runs tests → sees a failure → reads the error → fixes it → runs again ✓

Keep this light — you don't need to name the loop. The key idea is that the agent isn't just generating text, it's taking real actions and responding to what actually happens.

Section 02

Tokens, Prompts & Context Engineering

Three concepts that unlock better results from any AI tool — not just Gemini CLI.

What is a Token?

Think of tokens as the AI's unit of reading and writing — roughly one word per token.

The
quick
brown
fox
Why tokens matter
Every word you type in your prompt uses tokens. Every word the AI writes back uses tokens. There's a limit to how many can fit in one conversation — that limit is the context window.
Real-world sizes
A tweet ≈ 23 tokens
A full page of text ≈ 350 tokens
This entire slide deck ≈ ~5,000 tokens
Gemini's window: 1,000,000 tokens
Don't get into the weeds of tokenization here — the important concept is that there's a "memory limit" to each conversation, and Gemini's is unusually large.

The Context Window

The context window is the AI's working memory — how much it can "see" at once in a single conversation.

Gemini (ours!) 1,000,000 tokens
Claude 200,000 tokens
ChatGPT 128,000 tokens
Gemini's 1M token window fits: 7–9 novels · an entire codebase · a year of team messages — all in one conversation

The bigger the window, the more context you can give it — and the better the results.

The practical upside for tonight: you can paste your entire resume, your LinkedIn, multiple job descriptions, all at once. No need to worry about it "forgetting" earlier parts of the conversation.

How to Write a Good Prompt

The more context you give, the better the output. A vague ask gets a vague answer.

Role
"You are a friendly career coach who gives direct, practical advice…"
Context
"I have 5 years in project management and am transitioning into tech. Here is my resume summary…"
Task
"Rewrite my LinkedIn headline to highlight my technical skills."
Constraints
"Keep it under 220 characters. Don't use buzzwords like 'passionate' or 'ninja'."
Use a non-code example here to keep it accessible. The key lesson: Context is the most important part — most people skip it and wonder why they get generic results.

Your Cheat Sheet for Gemini: GEMINI.md

Create a file called GEMINI.md in your project folder. Gemini reads it automatically every time you start a session — so you don't have to re-explain yourself.

Think of it like a sticky note on your desk — "here's what I'm working on, here's how I like things done."
Takes 5 minutes to write. Saves you from explaining the same things on every prompt.
# What I'm working on
Personal portfolio website.
Plain HTML and CSS, no frameworks.

# My preferences
- Keep things simple and readable
- Mobile-friendly designs
- Explain what you changed and why

# Don't touch
# Leave the header section alone
# Don't add JavaScript
This is a really practical tip that people can use tonight. Encourage everyone to create one for their landing page project before they start Prompt 2.
Section 03

CLIs Can Access Your Computer

This is the part that surprises most people. The CLI isn't just answering questions — it's taking actions on your machine.

What Gemini CLI Can Do

Read & write your files
It can open, create, edit, and save files on your computer — not just suggest changes for you to copy-paste.
Run terminal commands
"Run my tests and fix anything that fails." It runs the command, reads the output, and tries again if something goes wrong.
Search the web
Google Search is built in. "Find the current salary range for this role in DC" — it looks it up live.
Control a browser New!
Can open Chrome, navigate pages, click buttons, fill in forms — all from a text instruction.
The "it actually writes the file" moment is usually where people's jaws drop. Emphasize that this isn't autocomplete — it's taking real actions on your machine.

Connecting to Your Other Tools

Gemini CLI can plug into the tools you already use — through a standard called MCP (think of it as a universal adapter, like USB-C for AI).

Gemini CLI
⇄
Adapter
⇄
GitHub
Google Drive
Slack
GitHub
"Open a pull request for my changes" — from the terminal, in plain English
Google Drive
"Summarize the documents in this folder" — without opening a browser
Slack / Jira
"Create a ticket for this bug I just found" — straight from your code session
MCP launched in Nov 2024 and was quickly adopted across the industry. The "USB-C" analogy lands well — one standard, works with everything. You don't need to set this up tonight, but it's good to know it exists.

A Quick Word on Safety

Because Gemini CLI can actually do things on your computer, it's worth knowing a few basics:

It will ask before doing things
By default, Gemini asks for your approval before writing or deleting any file. Read those prompts — they tell you exactly what it's about to do.
Don't paste passwords or private keys
Your prompts are sent to Google's servers. Stick to things you'd be okay sharing publicly.
It protects sensitive files
Gemini automatically avoids files that contain secrets like API keys. It's designed to be cautious by default.
Tonight is safe
We're reading files and creating new ones only — nothing gets deleted or overwritten.
Frame this as empowering, not scary. The approval prompts are actually a great learning tool — they show you exactly what an agentic CLI is doing step by step.

Installing Gemini CLI

Pause here and make sure everyone is following along. This is the most important prerequisite for the hands-on section — don't rush past it.

Step 1 — Install Node.js

Gemini CLI runs on Node.js — a free tool that lets you run JavaScript on your computer. You need version 20 or higher.

Mac
brew install node
No Homebrew? Download the .pkg installer from nodejs.org
Windows
choco install nodejs
No Chocolatey? Download the .msi installer from nodejs.org
Linux
curl -fsSL https://deb.nodesource.com/setup_24.x | sudo -E bash -
sudo apt-get install -y nodejs
Check it worked: node --version should print v20.x or higher
Give people a few minutes here. Drop the nodejs.org link in chat. Helpers should jump into breakout rooms or DMs for anyone stuck.

Step 2 — Install Gemini CLI

Once Node.js is installed, one command gets you Gemini CLI on any platform:


npm install -g @google/gemini-cli
Check it worked
gemini --version
You should see a version number like 0.37.0
First run
Type gemini and hit Enter. A browser tab will open asking you to sign in with Google — that's it, you're in!

Need help? Full platform-by-platform guide: gemini-install.md in this repo

Section 06 · Live Demo

Gemini CLI in Action

Switch to terminal now. Narrate every command before running it. Pause after each output to explain what the model did and why. Make tool calls visible — don't hide the inner workings.

Getting Started

Step 1: type gemini
A browser tab opens asking you to sign in with Google. That's it — no API key, no credit card.
Step 2: start chatting
You're now in an interactive session. Type naturally — it's a conversation, not a command.
Free tier
1,000 requests per day. More than enough for learning and experimentation. 

# Start Gemini (opens browser sign-in)
gemini

# Ask it something directly
gemini "What files are in this directory?"

# Give it a file to look at
gemini -f ./my-resume.txt "Summarize this"

# Ask it to do something
gemini "Create a simple HTML page
        that says hello world"
Walk through this live. Emphasise that it feels like texting or chatting with someone, not writing code. The -f flag for attaching files is the most important thing to learn tonight.

Handy Commands to Know

/tools
Shows everything Gemini can do in this session — great for exploring its capabilities
/stats
Shows how much of your conversation budget you've used so far
/quit
Exit the session (or just press Ctrl+D)
Pro tip: You can also use Gemini without the interactive session — just type gemini "your question here" and get an answer directly in the terminal.

Screenshot this slide to reference during the hands-on exercises →

Keep this simple — three commands is enough. The /tools command is especially nice because it shows participants what the agent can actually do without having to read docs.

Choosing Your Model

Gemini CLI gives you access to several models. You can switch between them any time during a session with /model.

Gemini 2.5 Flash Tonight
Fast, capable, and free. Great for everyday tasks, writing, research, and hands-on learning. This is what we'll use tonight.
Gemini 2.5 Pro
More powerful for complex reasoning and larger codebases. Available on the free tier with daily limits.
Other models
Flash-Lite and experimental variants are also available — useful for speed or trying cutting-edge features.
Switch models mid-session: type /model and pick from the list — your conversation history carries over.
Keep this quick. The key point is that /model is available if anyone wants to explore, but for tonight everyone stays on 2.5 Flash so the experience is consistent. Pro has stricter rate limits on the free tier which could cause frustration during the hands-on section.

Tips for Getting Great Results

Be specific
Instead of "improve my resume" → "rewrite my summary to emphasize leadership experience and target product manager roles"
Give it something to work with
Paste in your text, attach a file, or describe your situation. The more context, the better the output.
Tell it what to avoid
"Don't use corporate jargon" or "keep it under 3 sentences" — constraints make answers more useful.
Iterate, don't perfect
The first response is a starting point. Ask follow-up questions just like you would with a person.
These tips apply to any AI tool, not just Gemini CLI. Reinforce that this is a conversation — you wouldn't ask a human colleague one vague question and expect a perfect answer.
Hands-On · Prompt 1 of 3

System Insights

What happens
Gemini will actually look around your computer — checking what software is installed, spotting your projects, and giving you personalised recommendations. It's not guessing; it's genuinely looking.
Why start here
It's a great first prompt because you can fact-check the output yourself. You'll also get to watch it "think out loud" as it explores your machine.

Before you run it — what do you think it'll find? Take a guess. Compare after.

The "think out loud" observation is the key teaching moment — participants will see tool calls appearing in the output, which makes the agentic loop concrete and visible.
Prompt 1 of 3

System Insights

"Look at my development environment and tell me:
1. What languages and tools are installed, with exact version numbers
2. What projects or repositories do you see in my home directory
3. What would you recommend I update or clean up?

Be specific about versions and file paths."

Variation: add -f ~/.zshrc or -f ~/.bashrc to include your shell config in the context

This prompt uses the file system and shell tools — watch the tool calls in output. The more specific you ask it to be, the more it has to actually inspect (vs. make up answers).

Reflect & Refine

What surprised you?
Did it find tools or projects you'd forgotten? Did it miss anything obvious?
Context engineering question
What would you add to a GEMINI.md to make this kind of analysis run better every time?
Try a follow-up
"Now show me the git log for the most active project you found and summarize what's been happening."
5 minutes of reflection. The key insight: output quality = context quality. If Gemini missed something, it's usually because it couldn't see it — not because it couldn't reason about it.
Hands-On · Prompt 2 of 3

GitHub Landing Page

What this builds
A complete index.html personal site — deployable to GitHub Pages in under 2 minutes. No frameworks, no build step, just HTML and inline CSS.
Why GitHub Pages
Free hosting, custom domain support, deploys on push. A username.github.io URL you can put on a resume or send to a recruiter tonight.

Open LinkedIn now and copy your About section. No LinkedIn? Write 3–4 sentences describing yourself — that's enough context.

The "inline CSS, no dependencies" constraint is intentional — GitHub Pages is static, no build tooling. This constraint also forces simpler, more maintainable output.
Prompt 2 of 3

GitHub Landing Page

"Create a personal landing page for GitHub Pages based on this LinkedIn profile summary:

[PASTE YOUR LINKEDIN ABOUT SECTION HERE]

Requirements:
- Single index.html file with all CSS inline (no external stylesheets)
- Mobile-responsive, works without JavaScript
- Include: name, professional tagline, top skills, and contact links
- Professional but approachable tone
- Save the file to index.html in the current directory"
The "save the file" instruction at the end triggers Gemini to actually write to disk, not just print the HTML. This is the difference between a generative and an agentic interaction.

Deploy to GitHub Pages

  1. Create a new repo named exactly yourusername.github.io
  2. Gemini writes index.html directly to your working directory
  3. Commit and push to the main branch
  4. Go to repo Settings → Pages → Source: main, root folder
  5. Visit https://yourusername.github.io — live in ~60 seconds
Stuck on git? Ask Gemini: "Walk me through pushing this file to GitHub for the first time"
Steps 3–5 take under 2 minutes. Encourage everyone to share their live URL in chat. This is a concrete, shareable output people can send to recruiters tonight.

Keep Iterating

The first output is a starting point. The real skill is knowing how to refine.


"Add a projects section with 3 placeholder cards:
 title, description, tech stack chips, and a GitHub link"

"Change the color scheme to use #003380 for headings
 and #e81b39 for buttons and accents"

"Display the skills as pill badges instead of a bullet list,
 grouped by category"

"Add a dark mode using prefers-color-scheme media query"

"Make the header sticky and add a smooth-scroll anchor nav"

Each follow-up is a new context engineering exercise — be specific, state constraints, reference the existing code.

Model the iteration cycle — this is the core daily workflow of AI-assisted development. Natural language refinement is faster than manual CSS editing for most tasks here. Encourage 2–3 rounds.
Hands-On · Prompt 3 of 3

Career Research & Profile Review

What this does
Gemini uses its web search tool to research real job postings, cross-references your profile against actual role requirements, and produces specific, actionable recommendations — not generic advice.
Where the 1M context window helps
You can paste your entire resume, full LinkedIn About, and specific job postings all at once. The model reasons across everything together — no summarization loss.

Prepare: copy your LinkedIn About section + a 2–3 sentence resume summary. Full resume optional.

This prompt benefits most from specificity. Vague "software engineer" input gets vague output. The more concrete the profile, the more targeted the role research and suggestions.
Prompt 3 of 3

Career Research & Profile Review

"I'll share my LinkedIn profile and resume summary. Please:
1. Identify 5 specific job titles I should be targeting based on my background
2. For each title, name 3 companies in DC or fully remote that are actively hiring
3. List my top 3 skills gaps relative to those roles
4. Suggest 2 specific, concrete improvements to my LinkedIn headline
5. Suggest 2 specific improvements to my resume summary section

Use web search to find current job postings.

LinkedIn About: [PASTE HERE]
Resume summary: [PASTE HERE]"
The "use web search" instruction activates Gemini's search tool — it will pull real job postings rather than reasoning from training data. This is where the agentic loop becomes visible and valuable.

Privacy & Variations

Privacy: Prompt text is sent to Google's API. Use public LinkedIn info and resume summaries. Never paste passwords, compensation details, employer-confidential data, or SSNs.
Target a specific company
"Focus exclusively on roles at [Company]. What do their JDs emphasize that I should surface in my profile?"
Interview prep
"Generate 5 behavioral interview questions for a Senior DevOps Engineer role and help me draft STAR-format answers."
Salary research
"Search Levels.fyi and LinkedIn Salary for the compensation range for these roles in the DC metro area."
Full resume
Add -f ./resume.pdf (or paste as text) for deeper analysis across your complete work history.
Address privacy directly — it always comes up. The context window is sent per-request to Google's servers. Start with public info; escalate to more detail once you're comfortable. For salary data, asking Gemini to search Levels.fyi is more reliable than its training data.
